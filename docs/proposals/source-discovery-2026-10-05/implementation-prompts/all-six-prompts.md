## Phase-specific task

Create the foundations for three authorized changes: engine-specific query building; deeper metadata retrieval and ranking, including claim-aware previews; and expansion from strong seed papers. This phase prepares tested contracts and helpers without prematurely activating incomplete production behavior.

## 1. Map ownership and freeze a concrete specification

Trace initial planning and Round-1 execution in `agents/v2_initial_planner.py` and `researchassistant/research/v2_orchestrator.py`, adaptive execution in `agents/v2_adaptive_search.py`, discovery/Scout/clustering in `agents/v2_discovery.py`, acquisition/Probe in `agents/v2_acquisition.py`, selection in `agents/v2_source_selection.py`, provider adapters, source/trail/history readers, and API/CLI/desktop configuration. Confirm how `ResearchControls.sources_per_stance_per_round` currently works; do not repurpose it to mean metadata result depth.

Define one fresh discovery policy and explicit version identities shared by later phases. Keep historical policies supported. Initial implementation defaults should target 20 metadata results per logical query, maximum configurable 50; at most three physical pages per logical operation; 300 retained raw candidates per round and 1,000 per run; at most 60 candidates considered by Scout per round, further limited by safely available model budget; and the established acquisition cap of at most 25 clusters per round. For expansion, default to at most three seeds per run, one hop, ten unique neighbors per seed across relationship types, and thirty unique expansion candidates per run. These are independent ceilings, not allocations or guaranteed minima. Provider restrictions and smaller remaining budgets take precedence. Define a deterministic fair allocation for enabled directions/providers instead of exhausting the whole cap on whichever response finishes first. Tighten defaults if code-level budget analysis requires it, recording the reason and testing the result; do not loosen hard limits or misstate effective depth.

## 2. Typed representations required by all phases

Add narrowly owned, immutable, strict Pydantic contracts, reusing compatible existing ones, for:

- Discovery policy and per-provider capabilities, including search modes, field/operator support, metadata limits, pagination, identity lookup, references/citing/related-work support, and unsupported features.
- A conceptual query specification with required concept groups, bounded synonyms, optional methods/outcomes, purpose, enabled direction/provider, round, target Gap IDs, and application-owned identity. Keep the exact user claim separate.
- A compiled provider query/action: actual query text and sanitized parameters, mode, requested/effective depth, logical operation ID, compiler/policy identity, and fingerprint. Query actions and graph-neighbor actions must have distinct types; do not disguise graph requests as arbitrary search text.
- Provider attempt/page records: parent operation, sequence, bounds, status, response/result identity or hash, counts, timing where available, known/unknown cost basis, and typed failure. Credentials and credential-bearing URLs are never serialized.
- Raw and normalized discovery candidates; stable identity/grouping; explicit dispositions for duplicate, excluded, cap/budget prevented, not scouted, shortlisted, fetched, and unavailable cases; rank components and rationale; retained source locations without weakening current same-work identity rules.
- Preview requests/results: exact claim and permitted lane, snapshot ID/hash, exact offsets/text, section/context, relevance signals, content completeness/unknown classification, and versioned policy.
- Seed eligibility, work resolution, expansion actions/results/edges, pending or skipped outcomes, and provenance linking an expanded candidate to the seed, relationship, provider, round, and gaps.

Every accepted/failed record must bind to its run and application-owned identity. Specify meaningful unknown values instead of inventing quality, study design, full-text availability, edge verification, or independence. Reject contradictory caps, empty required concepts, invalid modes, malformed IDs, stale hashes, cross-run ownership, cycles where prohibited, and conflicting completed replays. Keep provider relevance scores separate; they are not comparable across providers by assumption.

## 3. Persistence, budgets, identity, and compatibility

Add typed store helpers and dispatch/registries needed for new artifact families, plus the minimum historical/API readers required to inspect them. Plan durable per-operation and per-page checkpoints and explicit interrupted/unknown outcomes. Reservations must be durable before transport. With no provider idempotency/recovery guarantee, a restart must not blindly repeat an unknown request or claim no charge; retain exposure, show an explicit incomplete outcome, and require a new separately reserved attempt if recovery policy permits one.

Bind the policy, capabilities/compiler versions, candidate-ranking/preview/seed versions, effective settings, changed prompt schemas, and provider configuration to fresh run fingerprints and packaged source identity. Keep old-client handling explicit: distinguish missing historical fields from selected new settings. Do not deserialize unknown policy identities through permissive current defaults.

Protect downstream model capacity before admitting more Scout, search-planning, or selection work. Graph metadata calls and search pages consume the applicable provider request/cost budgets. New audit counters distinguish logical queries, HTTP requests, metadata records, unique work candidates, acquisition attempts, usable snapshots, and admitted evidence. Atomic reservation and ownership must survive concurrent operations and cancellation. Do not store unrelated runs in an unbounded process cache.

## Acceptance

Test policy lower/upper bounds, capability rejection, unknown metadata, stable identity/order, serialization round trips, payload-hash corruption, cross-run and conflicting replay rejection, reservation races, interrupted starts, zero/near-exhausted budgets, new/old-client defaults, and compatible history reads without mutation. Demonstrate legacy fingerprints/artifacts retain their meaning and incompatible new settings cannot resume an old run. The application continues using its prior runtime policy until later integrated phases explicitly activate the new one. Deliver a concrete foundation inventory and the exact contracts later prompts must use; no required contract or accounting owner may remain a TODO.

## Phase-specific task

Prerequisite: inspect and verify Phase 1's contracts, budget hooks, identity rules, and plan. Implement useful query construction for each existing enabled discovery engine and wire it into both initial and adaptive planning. Do not assume a feature works because an adapter has an unused field.

## 1. Separate conceptual planning from provider syntax

Extend/version the initial Planner and adaptive Search Agent handoffs so the model proposes bounded concepts and aliases appropriate to an application-owned lane. Prefer concise required concept groups and optional evidence-method/outcome groups over long sentences requiring every word. Preserve raw claim, claim-component scope, provider eligibility, direction, round, original Gap IDs, and the established initial/per-round lane limits. The application compiles the final executable request deterministically; models cannot invent endpoint parameters, providers, budgets, IDs, queries in disabled lanes, or unbounded synonym lists.

Version and implement executable prompt changes in `prompts/v2_initial_planner.md` and `prompts/search_agent.md` or explicitly versioned successors, including matching response schemas, rejection feedback, prompt loaders/identities, and precise examples. Keep retained historical paths dispatchable. Reuse existing Planner/Search Agent selected routes and physical-call accounting rather than adding a free query-rewrite model call.

## 2. Build a documented capability-specific compiler

Read current official primary documentation while implementing; record URLs and verification dates. Relevant starting points are:

- OpenAlex: https://help.openalex.org/api/searching/ and https://help.openalex.org/api/semantic-search/
- arXiv: https://info.arxiv.org/help/api/user-manual.html
- PubMed/E-utilities: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK25499/ and official NCBI alternatives if that page is unavailable.
- Exa: https://exa.ai/docs/reference/search
- The existing SERP provider and any enabled Serper adapter's actual official API documentation/configuration.

For OpenAlex, implement supported phrase/Boolean concept searches and a genuine semantic request mode. The existing adapter's `request.semantic` switch is currently unused by ordinary orchestration; connect it through the new frozen policy. Respect documented field/mode/filter restrictions. Semantic, lexical, filtered lookup, and any optional upstream rerank may have different prices/limits. Do not silently enable paid upstream reranking or apply lexical pricing to other modes. Provide a budget-safe lexical fallback only as a distinct recorded/reserved attempt when policy allows.

For arXiv, compile explicit fielded/Boolean expressions with correct escaping, rather than prepending `all:` to an arbitrary natural-language sentence. For PubMed, produce supported fielded/Boolean term combinations without inventing MeSH mappings or invalid tags. For Exa, provide concise natural-language intent and only supported structured parameters; do not force a scholarly Boolean language into semantic web search. For SERP-style search, support verified search operators/phrases and avoid unsupported API parameters. Existing provider choices stay user-controlled: topic suitability informs wording and ranking but never silently enables a disabled provider or drops an enabled lane.

Keep aliases bounded and justified, e.g. ALPR/LPR/ANPR and full phrases where appropriate. Do not add an exhaustive acronym alone if it causes broad ambiguity. Avoid biased exclusions such as removing null results or evidence contradicting a support-directed query. Date, language, publication type, and authority restrictions require explicit documented policy; do not arbitrarily filter away older foundational studies.

## 3. Validate execution and novelty

Carry requested/effective settings and compiled parameters into provider requests and durable audit artifacts, and freeze them in identity. Enforce URL/parameter/query length, reserved characters, untrusted content, and unsupported mode restrictions before transport. Query novelty must understand material conceptual/mode changes rather than treating all engine-specific compilations as token shuffles. Preserve safeguards against duplicate/trivial adaptive proposals, and never spend extra calls merely to fill a discarded slot.

Use one shared query execution/validation owner for Round 1 and later rounds; remove duplicated hardcoded query construction carefully. Provider failure must remain typed, bounded, cancellable, and accounted. Do not silently change a rejected semantic query into lexical search and report semantic success. Keep diagnostic display text separate from executable query identity.

## Acceptance

Cover phrases, Unicode, quotes, AND/OR precedence, synonyms, punctuation, long/ambiguous claims, unsupported features, lexical/semantic restrictions, missing provider configuration, rejection repair, duplicate proposals, partial provider failure, retries and cancellation, caps, request cost basis, and frozen resume compatibility. Include ALPR crime and discrimination cases, a biomedical case, a non-scholarly normative claim, and disabled-direction/provider cases. Prove initial and adaptive calls use the compiler and semantic mode can be deliberately exercised under its correct budget. Existing unsupported modes must have clear behavior. No paid requests.
